Anatomy of a Safe Asset: Bund Market Structure and Reform

Abstract

This paper constructs a non-anonymous transaction-level dataset for German Bunds, the euro area’s benchmark safe asset, from regulatory reports since 2008. We show how this safe asset actually trades: activity is organized around a concentrated dealer core, segmented across investor sectors, and fragmented across venues. In stress, identified flows reveal who supplies the safe asset and who absorbs it: foreign official accounts sell Bunds whether prices fall in a dash for cash or rise in a flight to quality; hedge funds buy in both episodes, while primary dealers absorb Covid selling but distribute Bunds after fiscal supply news. Hedge funds and official sector accounts carry the most permanent price-relevant flow, yet pay middling effective half-spreads, while retail households pay by far the highest effective half-spreads: execution costs do not increase monotonically with the price content of flow. We evaluate two reforms that target this structure: mandatory central clearing would cut dealers’ net settlement obligations in Bunds by 35%, less than comparable estimates for U.S. Treasuries and U.K. Gilts; in the benchmark structural calibration, platform centralization lowers welfare: the matching gains from broader dealer access do not offset the loss of relationship surplus under imperfect dealer competition.
